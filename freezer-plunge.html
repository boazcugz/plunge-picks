Why I tried something different

The cold was easy.
The ice runs? Not so much.

Cold plunging with ice can work. But a daily routine can become a cycle of buying, freezing, carrying and replacing ice. I wanted water that would be ready without starting that process again every afternoon.

Ice is temporaryEvery session needs another cooling plan, especially in warm weather.
Preparation adds frictionWhen setup becomes a chore, it's easier to skip a day.
Chillers are another investmentPurpose-built systems are worth comparing, but they weren't my low-budget experiment.
My answer: a used freezer with an Inkbird temperature controller. I spent about $300 and have used it daily for six months. The trade-off is significant electrical and water-safety risk, so consider this an owner report — not a recommendation to copy the conversion. See the actual parts and limitations ↓

Important: Chest freezers are not designed as plunge tubs. Water and mains power are a serious hazard. A panel RCD helps reduce electric-shock risk but doesn't make immersion in an appliance safe. Disconnect all power before entry and have a qualified electrician assess your installation. This isn't a wiring or conversion tutorial.

Curious how it actually runs? My six-month report

The second-hand Fujicom is somewhere around 300–400 litres (I never checked the label). I keep the Inkbird ITC-308 set to 3°C with the probe in the water. In the photo it reads 6.1°C after someone got out, then cools back down. I rinse first, skim the surface with an aquarium net and replace the water about every two weeks. The things people actually asked

Questions from the Reddit thread

Answers about my own setup, not claims about every freezer.

Does it run 24/7?

Yes. It's connected through the Inkbird around the clock. The compressor switches on only when the water warms past the setting. I unplug it whenever someone is in the water.

Did you seal the freezer?

No extra sealing. Its original drain plug held, and I've had no leaks in six months. That's specific to my freezer; inspect yours and check for leaks before use.

What about GFCI protection?

My home has an RCD at the electrical panel. RCDs and GFCIs both detect certain current leakage faults, but protection and installation requirements differ by location. It is not a substitute for disconnecting power.

Isn't the lock dangerous?

Mine locks with a key. It's locked when nobody is around so my small kids can't get in. The lid stays fully open while someone is inside.

How do you clean the water?

No filtration. I rinse before getting in, skim debris using an aquarium net and change the water every two weeks.

How much electricity does it use?

Not measured yet. My rough calculation is around $5–8 per month, but that is an estimate, not a real usage reading. Actual costs depend on weather, insulation and electricity rates.

Can this go in a basement?

General planning advice, not something I've tested myself: think about splashing, drainage and water damage. A cooler room may reduce compressor runtime. Check ventilation, floor drainage and the electrical setup with a qualified professional.

Buying a used freezer?

Five things I'd inspect before paying for one.

  • Drain plug and any signs of previous leaks
  • Rust or corrosion on the inside
  • Condition of the lid gasket
  • Whether the lid has a working lock
  • Whether the freezer actually cools
