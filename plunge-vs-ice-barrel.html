Plunge All-In vs Ice Barrel 300: Which Cold Plunge Is Right for You?

Updated July 2026 · Research-based comparison by Plunge Picks · 6 min read

These two products sit at opposite ends of the cold plunge market, which is exactly why people cross-shop them. The Plunge All-In is a fully automated, chiller-driven tub that keeps cold water waiting for you at the push of a button. The Ice Barrel 300 is a simpler, upright barrel you cool with ice — at roughly a seventh of the price. This is a comparison of what each one actually is, not which is "better" in the abstract, because the right answer depends entirely on your budget, space, and how committed you already are.

Short version: If you plunge often, want zero ice hassle, and the budget is there, the Plunge All-In is the more capable machine. If you're starting out or want the lowest-cost way in — with the option to add a chiller later — the Ice Barrel 300 is the smarter first purchase.

The Plunge All-In

Plunge All-In Premium

This is the "set it and forget it" end of cold plunging. An integrated chiller holds the water down to a manufacturer-listed 37°F continuously, and onboard ozone sanitation plus a filtration pump keep the water clean — the manufacturer states it cycles the full volume roughly every 15 minutes. There's no ice to buy, haul, or time around. The trade-offs are price, weight (about 238 lbs empty and over 1,000 lbs filled, so it's effectively permanent once placed), and the need for a dedicated 120V circuit.

Pros

  • No ice — cold water is always ready
  • Continuous filtration and ozone sanitation
  • Optional heater for contrast therapy

Cons

  • Premium price (~$7,990 listed)
  • Very heavy — essentially non-portable
  • Needs a dedicated electrical circuit

The Ice Barrel 300

Ice Barrel 300 Entry-level

The Ice Barrel 300 is an upright, insulated barrel you sit in, cooled the old-fashioned way with ice. At about 61 lbs empty it's genuinely movable, it's made in the USA from recycled materials, and the manufacturer lists a limited lifetime warranty. Crucially, it's "chiller-ready" — it ships with bulkhead ports so you can add a chiller later and convert it into a no-ice setup without buying a whole new tub. That upgrade path is the reason a lot of buyers start here.

Pros

  • Far lower entry price (~$1,150 listed)
  • Light enough to move; upright footprint
  • Chiller-ready — upgrade later without replacing it

Cons

  • Ice-only out of the box — ongoing ice cost and effort
  • No active filtration until you add a chiller
  • Temperature depends on how much ice you add

The verdict

This isn't a case of one product beating the other — they're built for different buyers, and the roughly $6,800 price gap is doing most of the talking. What you're really paying for with the Plunge All-In is automation: no ice, always-cold water, and filtration. What you keep with the Ice Barrel 300 is money and flexibility: a low entry price and a path to add cooling later.

Choose the Plunge All-In if…

  • You already plunge several times a week and know you'll keep going
  • You never want to deal with buying or making ice
  • You have the budget and a permanent spot with a dedicated outlet
  • Clean, always-ready water matters more than portability

Choose the Ice Barrel 300 if…

  • You're newer to cold plunging and don't want to risk $8,000 on a habit you're still testing
  • You want the option to add a chiller down the road
  • You'd rather have something light you can move
  • Upfront cost is the deciding factor
A middle path: Because the Ice Barrel 300 is chiller-ready, some buyers start with the barrel and ice, confirm the habit sticks, then add a chiller later — reaching a no-ice setup for less than a premium all-in-one.
