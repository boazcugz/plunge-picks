Shower measurement system
How do I find my numbers? ＋

Measure the cold tap with a thermometer once the temperature is steady. For flow, collect water for 10 seconds and multiply its volume by six to get liters or US gallons per minute.

This estimates a continuous supply of fresh water. It does not model a pre-chilled tank or recirculated water.

Example temperatures and run times are for planning, not exposure recommendations.

A showerhead isn’t a chiller.

Peek at the cool science ＋

Ice shower calculation

Water use = flow × run time. Continuous cooling power = (flow in L/min ÷ 60) × 4.186 × (tap °C − target °C), in kW. Water is approximated as 1 kg/L. Heat removed = water liters × 4.186 × temperature drop, in kJ.

This is thermal cooling duty, not electrical consumption or a product rating. The ideal estimate excludes heat gains and equipment losses; actual output depends on operating conditions. It does not calculate a safe exposure time.

Based on OpenStax’s heat-capacity relationship.

Ice calculation

We balance the heat lost by the water against the energy needed to warm the ice to 0°C, melt it, and warm the meltwater to your target. Water is approximated as 1 kg per liter. Leave room for that extra water.

m = V × 4.186 × (Tstart − Ttarget) ÷ [2.09 × (−Tice) + 334 + 4.186 × Ttarget]. Temperatures in °C; mass in kg.

Constants: water 4.186 and ice 2.09 kJ/kg·°C; melting 334 kJ/kg. Heat capacity reference · Melting reference.

Cost comparison

Ice cost = sessions/week × 52 × bags/session × bag price. Chiller running cost = daily kWh × 365 × electricity price + 12 × monthly upkeep.

We add your setup cost once. Simple payback divides it by the monthly operating savings. It assumes year-round use and excludes financing, resale, repairs beyond entered upkeep, and the value of your time.
