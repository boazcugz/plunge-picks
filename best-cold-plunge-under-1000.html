Best Cold Plunge Under $1,000: What Your Money Actually Buys

Prices checked on sellers' own pages on 7 October 2026 · Research-based guide by PlungeWise · 5 min read

Under $1,000 you have three real choices: an ice tub you fill from bags or molds, a budget chiller bundle, or a DIY chiller build. Most ready-made chiller tubs cost several times that. We have not tested any of the products below; everything here comes from what each seller publishes.

Short version: if you will plunge only a few times a week, an ice tub is the cheapest way in. If you plunge most days, run your numbers in the ice vs. chiller calculator: ice can cost more than a budget chiller within a year. The Ice Barrel 300, often listed in "under $1,000" roundups, is $1,149.99 plus $300 shipping on its own site.

1. The Cold Pod: the cheapest ready-made start

An insulated, inflatable 85-gallon tub with a thermal cover. The listing gives an outer size of 29.5 × 29.5 × 29.5 inches and no chiller. You add ice for every session, so the real cost is the ice. Use the ice bath calculator to see how many bags your water needs.

2. Stock tank: the most water for the money

A galvanized livestock tank holds 100 gallons or more and has nothing to break. It is uninsulated metal, so ice melts faster than in an insulated tub. Our budget guide compares it with The Cold Pod in more detail.

3. Plunge Chill tub + chiller: the only chiller bundle we found under $1,000

Plunge Chill sells a 92-gallon insulated tub with a 1/3 hp chiller for $569, shown as a sale from $1,199. It lists:

  • Water down to 39°F
  • A 600 W compressor, 39–45 dB, and running costs of "approximately $1/day" (the seller's estimate)
  • A built-in pump and filter
  • A 2-year warranty and a 30-day return, minus return shipping

Two things to weigh. First, a 1/3 hp chiller is less than half the size of the 0.8 hp and larger units in $3,000+ tubs, so expect it to struggle more in a hot room or summer sun. Second, a "52% off" price shown as standard should be treated as the real price, not a deal that is about to end.

4. DIY chiller: cheapest chiller, most work

An aquarium or hydroponics chiller, plus a pump and filter, can cool a tub you already own. It takes plumbing and careful electrical work next to water. Our DIY guide, Path B covers the parts and sizing. We advise against the popular chest-freezer conversion; the same guide explains why.

Why the Ice Barrel 300 is not on this list

Ice Barrel's own site lists the 300 at $1,149.99, with $300 shipping to the contiguous US. It is an ice-cooled, upright barrel with chiller-ready ports and a limited lifetime warranty. It is a reasonable buy, but not under $1,000. See Plunge All-In vs Ice Barrel 300.

Ice or chiller: the number that decides it

A chiller under $1,000 pays for itself only if you would otherwise buy a lot of ice. Plunge four times a week with two $5 bags each, and ice costs about $2,080 a year. Plunge twice a week with one bag, and it is about $520. Try your own numbers:
